What is ADHD, really?

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition that can affect the way someone regulates attention, activity, and impulses. It often begins in childhood, but many people are not recognized until adolescence or adulthood. ADHD can look different from person to person: one person may daydream and lose track of details, while another may feel restless, talk quickly, or act before thinking.

The word “attention” can be misleading. People with ADHD are usually able to focus deeply when a task feels new, urgent, meaningful, or rewarding. The challenge is often directing and shifting attention consistently, especially when a task is repetitive or has a distant payoff. Working memory, time awareness, organization, sleep, and emotional regulation can be part of the picture too.

ADHD is not a measure of intelligence, care, or potential. Helpful support may include practical systems, environmental changes, coaching, therapy, medication, or some combination. A qualified clinician is the right person to assess symptoms and consider other explanations. This quiz is a private starting point for reflection, not a medical diagnosis.

How We Can Help

Our website is designed to be a screening and awareness tool, not a diagnostic platform. The 18-question assessment is intended to help you reflect on experiences that may be associated with ADHD and understand whether seeking professional guidance could be helpful.

Your results do not mean that you have ADHD, and they cannot replace an evaluation by a qualified mental-health professional. Only a trained professional can properly assess and diagnose ADHD.

Our main goal is to make taking the first step easier. If your responses suggest that professional guidance may be beneficial, our platform can help you find and connect with therapists in your locality. We also provide supportive tools such as a mood tracker, To-Do list, and Pomodoro timer to help you manage your daily routine.
